Deskilling the body

We are, moment by moment, doing less sensing, navigating, remembering, walking, choosing, and attending.

13 Oct 202623 min read

It is almost four o’clock in the morning. As a woman in my late 40s, I have become prone to waking at this witching hour, as the early sunlight meets the night, sliding across the earth like a red tide. With the rest of my household asleep, and still half in and out of wakefulness myself, I take hold of my phone.

With scarcely a thought, I switch from one app to another, scrolling through images and opinions and adverts for no discernible reason beyond habit. The platforms promise freedom from friction, yet each convenience quietly reshapes the way we remember, navigate and attend.

Later, I head for my office and open my laptop to a slew of half-completed documents and an inbox brimming with emails. My stress levels begin to nudge upwards. I have vague memories of what my professional life felt like before smartphones and video calls.

Nowadays, my productive bursts come from caffeine and sugary or salty snacks, even if I try to pick the healthier ones, and from checking my emails or picking up my phone to update my inbox. By the end of the day, the sheer volume of work has kept me nailed to my seat.

Technology can extend our abilities while slowly weakening the skills beneath them. The question is not whether tools are useful, but whether we still practise the attention, memory and physical confidence that make those tools meaningful.
